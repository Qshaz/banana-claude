# Tadabur Reflection Framework

These questions guide the user from understanding the verse intellectually to feeling it personally and acting on it. Never dump all questions in one go. Select 3–4 per session, tailored to the specific verse and what the user shared about their situation. Ask one or two at a time, then pause for the user's response.

---

## Layer 1 — Understanding the verse

These questions open the session. Use one or two to help the user engage with the text itself.

- "What word or phrase in this verse stays with you?"
- "What does Allah say directly in this verse? Can you put it in your own words?"
- "When was this verse revealed, and what was happening? What does that context tell you?"
- "Which attribute of Allah appears in this verse? What does that name mean to you personally?"
- "Is there a promise in this verse? What exactly is being promised, and to whom?"

---

## Layer 2 — Connecting to your life

These questions bridge the verse to the user's present situation.

- "How does this verse speak to where you are right now?"
- "If Allah said this verse specifically to you today, what would you hear?"
- "Is there someone in your life this verse makes you think of?"
- "Does this verse comfort you, challenge you, or both? Why?"
- "What does your heart feel when you sit with this verse?"
- "Is there a fear or hope in your life right now that this verse speaks to?"

---

## Layer 3 — The personal message

These questions help the user receive what Allah is saying *to them* through this verse.

- "What do you think Allah is trying to tell you through this verse today — specifically, given what you're going through?"
- "The scholars say [tafsir insight]. What does that mean for your situation?"
- "The verse was revealed at a time when [context from tafsir]. How is your situation similar?"
- "If you believed this verse completely — not just intellectually, but in your bones — what would change?"
- "What is the verse asking you to let go of?"
- "What is it asking you to hold onto?"

---

## Layer 4 — Action and transformation

These questions move the user from feeling to doing.

- "What is one thing this verse is calling you to do differently this week?"
- "What would it look like to live this verse on a hard day?"
- "Is there something you've been putting off that this verse is nudging you toward?"
- "Who in your life needs to hear or see this verse through you?"
- "What would it feel like, one year from now, to look back and say 'that verse changed something in me'?"

---

## Layer 5 — Dialogue with Allah (du'a prompts)

These questions help the user turn the verse into personal supplication.

- "In light of this verse, what do you want to ask Allah for right now?"
- "What would you say to Allah if you were speaking directly to Him about what you're going through?"
- "The verse says Allah is [Attribute]. How does that change the way you speak to Him today?"
- "What do you need most from Allah right now that this verse suggests He can give?"

---

## Layer 6 — Closing the session

Use one of these to wrap up and invite journaling.

- "What do you want to remember from today's session, one month from now?"
- "If you could hold just one word or phrase from this verse in your heart this week, what would it be?"
- "What have you understood about yourself through this verse today?"

---

## Personalisation rules

**If the user mentioned job loss / financial worry:**
Anchor Layer 2 questions to provision, rizq, security. Example: "Allah promises that every creature's rizq is His responsibility. What would it feel like to hand your worry about work over to that promise?"

**If the user mentioned relationship pain (divorce, betrayal, gossip):**
Anchor to dignity, justice, Allah seeing what people do. Example: "The scholars note that this verse was often recited by those who were wronged but had no worldly recourse. Where do you find yourself in that?"

**If the user mentioned illness or loss:**
Lead with gentleness and validation before reflection. Anchor to Allah's closeness and Ayyub's example. Never imply the illness is punishment.

**If the user mentioned childlessness:**
Treat with particular care. Lead with Zakariyya's and Hannah's stories. Acknowledge that the desire for children is itself a form of worship. Avoid platitudes.

**If the user seems in acute distress:**
Skip the intellectual layers entirely. Go straight to Layer 3 and Layer 5. The verse and du'a may be what they need most right now.

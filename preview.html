What do my books say about healing from trauma?

Across your library, several books converge on a powerful insight: healing from trauma is not about erasing the past, but about changing your relationship with it.

Src 1 Bessel van der Kolk argues in The Body Keeps the Score that trauma is stored physically in the body, not just in memory — which is why talk therapy alone often isn't enough. He emphasises movement, breath work, and body-awareness practices as essential pathways.

Src 2 Brené Brown in Daring Greatly connects healing to vulnerability — the courage to be seen despite pain. She writes that shame compounds trauma, and that speaking our story to someone who has earned the right to hear it is a critical turning point.

Src 3 Viktor Frankl offers a different lens: even in extreme suffering, finding meaning transforms the experience. "When we are no longer able to change a situation, we are challenged to change ourselves."
